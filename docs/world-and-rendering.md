# World and rendering

Detail for Milestones 3 and 5 in [PLAN.md](../PLAN.md). Summary: `Game` loads Fort Boxy offline. Its sky, fog, ambient light, floor brightness, base marker and castle textures now match the Wine version. Open: the floor's slightly lower saturation, `renderLevel` not yet applied to `QualitySettings`, SSAO on Metal, and the laser aimer colour close up.

## Fort Boxy loading

Status (2026-10-18): `Game` now loads Fort Boxy (`Whirlds/FortBoxy/Fort Boxy.unity`) additively in place of `FallbackGround`. Fort Boxy is the first entry in the original `Game` scene's built-in world list, which the game used when the update server was unreachable, so it is the world an offline original build opens on. `World` loads the scene, makes it the active scene so its skybox, fog, and ambient light apply, then repeats the standard-component checks from `Game.js` `Init()`: find `Base` (or instantiate `Resources/Base`), collect terrains and `Sea`, and instantiate `Resources/Floor` and `Resources/Light` when the world has none. The player spawn waits for the load. `FallbackGround` remains only as a last resort when no ground is found under `Base`.

Verified in the Unity `6000.3.26f1` editor on macOS `27.0.1`: both `Init -> InitGame -> Game` and direct `Game` entry log `[World] Loaded world: Fort Boxy`, spawn the buggy on the castle beside `Base`, create no fallback ground, and log no errors. The chase camera starts at y = 10000 and descends to the buggy; it only does so while the editor is advancing frames (an unfocused editor or locked screen leaves the Game view showing flat fog colour).

## Base marker

Base marker (2026-10-19): `BaseSimpleModern` ports `BaseSimple.js`, whose missing script left the base marker's `Outer` and `Inner` cylinders at their smallest size. They now grow with camera distance (0.5 to 10 times), spin, and scroll their texture. `World` attaches it when the world loads. Verified in the editor: the marker is visible from the far camera.

## Parity fixes from a Wine comparison (2026-10-19)

- Sky: `Settings` now sets the camera's far clip as `Settings.js` did (`worldViewDist` 5000, capped by the saved `viewCap`) and derives `worldFog` from it, so `CameraVehicle` clears to the skybox beyond 2000 m. `World` keeps the `Game` scene's skybox if the world scene brings none. Verified in the editor: the `Space` skybox shows and the far clip is 5000.
- Chase camera: `Settings` now loads the saved camera preferences on start as `Settings.js` `getPrefs()` did (`cam`, `camChase` default 1, `camDist`, `flightCam`, `gyroCam`, `renderLevel`, `viewCap`). The `Game` scene had serialised `camChase` 0 (Smooth), whose world-space `Vector3.one` offset left the camera diagonally behind; the original default is 1 (Agile), directly behind along the velocity. Verified in the editor: the camera sits level and directly behind while driving straight.
- Base marker: `BaseMarkerModern` ports `WhirldLOD.js` as configured on `Resources/Base.prefab` (`LOD0`/`LOD1`, `lodLevMax` 3), so at the default render level the `LOD1` cylinders hide and `LOD0` shows. `LOD0`'s legacy particle emitter, which Unity no longer supports, is rebuilt as a `ParticleSystem` from the original values (read from the Unity 4 prefab's type tree): 20 per second, 5 to 7 s life, `Lightning` additive material (its own copy with untiled texture, since `BaseSimpleModern` tiles the cylinder's copy), alpha fading in and out, vertical random force, at most 0.3 of the screen. Rebuilt from the legacy formulas on 2026-10-20 (see the legacy particle formulas under the laser aimer): the animator values (`sizeGrow` 1, `damping` 0.7, `rndForce` (0, 5, 0)) and emitter values (`rndVelocity` (0.3, 1, 0.3), ellipsoid 0.1, size 1) were re-read from the prefab. The original update loop at 0x161f30 also shows how `rndForce` is applied: each frame, after damping, each axis gets a fresh uniform value in ±`rndForce` × dt. Since lifetimes vary between 5 and 7 s, no size-over-lifetime curve can give every particle `(1 + sizeGrow)^t`, so `BaseMarkerModern` applies the original per-frame step to each particle in `LateUpdate`: velocity × 0.7^dt, plus the random force, and size 1 × 2ᵗ m (up to 128 m, capped at 0.3 of the screen). The earlier hand-tuned growth (about 20 times) and the omitted damping are gone. Checked in the Unity `6000.3.26f1` editor on macOS `27.0.1`: logged sizes follow 2ᵗ exactly (2.73 m at 1.45 s, 46.8 m at 5.55 s), and particles stay within about 3 m of the emitter. Compared side by side with the Wine version from a similar chase view beside the pad: the glow's width and brightness match, but the Wine column is taller, reaching from the floor to the top of the view, where the port shows a shorter oval. The likely cause is frame rate: the random force is redrawn every frame and scaled by dt, so the spread grows with frame time, and the Wine version ran at 12 FPS against the editor's much higher rate. The original therefore looked different at different frame rates; the port follows the original formula and has not been tuned to the 12 FPS look. Not yet compared: the Wine version at a higher frame rate, and brightness of the white core close up.

## Shared-material rendering workaround

Rendering workaround (2026-10-19): in Unity `6000.3.26f1` on Metal, Fort Boxy's AirCastle renderers that share the `Object` material drew with the wrong texture, or not at all, depending on camera distance and draw order. The spawn slab looked like the floor and the hanging pyramids vanished. A material copy per renderer draws them correctly; a single shared fresh copy does not. Ruled out: shader (`Diffuse Fast` and `Diffuse` behave the same), lightmaps, occlusion data, dynamic batching (already off), mesh bounds and mipmaps. The likely cause is the legacy meshes' differing vertex layouts across draws that share a material, but this is unconfirmed. `World` gives every world renderer its own material copy after loading. Verified in the editor at near and far camera distances.

## World textures

World textures (2026-10-19): in Fort Boxy as captured in this repository, 880 `Castle` and `CenterRamp` cubes used Unity's white default material, and 48 `Castle` pyramids had no mesh, so they were neither drawn nor solid. The original builds these primitives at load time in `WhirldIn.js`, using the Whirld `Object` hex material, and its `TextureObject()` gives them world-space UVs (one texture repeat per metre). `World` now restores both. Primitives named `cube`, `pyramid`, `cone`, or `mesh` that are still on a default material get the `Object` material from `Resources/Cube` and a port of `TextureObject()`. Pyramids without a mesh get a generated one, with a matching mesh collider. The source `Pyramid.blend` imports only when Blender is installed, and its converted FBX is empty. The generated mesh has the `.blend` geometry at half size: base corners at ±0.5 and the apex at (0, 0.5, 0). At that size the pyramids sit flush on the surrounding geometry: tower bases rest on the ground, and the spire bases meet the roof tops at y = 85. Verified in the Unity `6000.3.26f1` editor: the castle shows the same hex pattern everywhere, as in the Wine version, and the pyramids are drawn.

## Lighting

Lighting (2026-10-19): Fort Boxy rendered at about half the Wine version's brightness (orange floor about 62–67 against 110–120 in screenshots). The built-in legacy shaders of Unity 4 and earlier (the original shipped on them; `unity/` is on 4.6.1f1) finished with `texture * lighting DOUBLE`, so lights and ambient light counted twice. Unity 5 dropped the doubling. After the world loads, `World` doubled every light's intensity and the ambient colour once. Superseded on 2026-10-20: with the original ambient restored, no doubling is needed (see Fort Boxy housekeeping below). Fort Boxy's only light is the `Resources/Light` directional light (intensity 1). Ambient is flat 0.2 and the colour space is Gamma, as in the original. The 0.2 ambient comes from the scene in this repository. Downloaded worlds set their own ambient light (`rndAmbientLight` in `.utw` files), and the author's example exports use about 0.5–0.66. Fort Boxy's real value is unknown, because its world file is not in the repository history or the Wine install. At the time, the floor measured about 116–131, apparently matching the Wine version.

Open, low priority (2026-10-20): check the scene lighting settings that the Unity 5 and Unity 6 upgrades may have changed silently. Unity 5 changed the default ambient source (flat colour to skybox) and added ambient intensity, changed point and spot light attenuation and intensity handling, and replaced Beast lightmaps with Enlighten. Compare each world light's intensity, range and attenuation, and the scene ambient and fog settings, between the `pre-unity6-upgrade` tag and now, and treat any value the upgrade changed as suspect. Legacy particle behaviour is not affected: it was frozen from Unity 3.5 onwards, and the laser aimer's formulas come from the original player.

## Fort Boxy housekeeping (2026-10-20)

- Layers: world objects stay on layer 0, which matches the original. The loop in `Game.js` `Init()` that moved layer-0 objects to layer 8 is commented out, and `WhirldIn.js` assigns no layers. So the camera and minimap layer-8 tests never hit Fort Boxy geometry in the original either. No change made.
- Fog, ambient light and floor texture (corrected 2026-10-20): the original world file is still published at `http://map.marsxplr.com.s3.amazonaws.com/FortBoxy.utw` (11,793 bytes, last modified 13 September 2009). A copy is kept at `reference/whirlds/FortBoxy.utw`. Its only world parameters are `marsxplr` and `message`. It has no `rndFogColor`, `rndFogDensity`, `rndAmbientLight` or floor `Texture`; the Whirld exporter writes the `rnd*` lines only when the author had fog switched on. `WhirldIn.js` built the world inside the `Game` scene, so the original used the `Game` scene's settings: fog on, colour (0.04, 0, 0.12), density from `worldFog`, flat ambient about 0.66. The floor used the `Floor` prefab's default texture. The port had applied the captured scene's fog off and ambient 0.2 instead. `World` now keeps the `Game` scene's fog and ambient settings after making Fort Boxy the active scene. The 0.66 ambient was confirmed in the Unity 4-era `unity/Assets/_Scenes/Game.unity`.

  With the original fog and ambient, the port needs no lighting compensation. `World` no longer doubles lights or ambient light. Remaining difference: the floor's mean colour is (255, 77, 28) in the Wine version and (227, 78, 41) in the port, so the port's orange is slightly less saturated. Not yet investigated. In both versions the horizon meets the starfield sharply. `Tools -> MarsXPLR -> Log Scene Look` also logs the screen brightness.

  Rendering effects (2026-10-20): the port never applied the "Rendering Effects" block of `Settings.js` `updatePrefs()`, so the camera's `ContrastStretchEffect` stayed on at every quality level. That effect keeps re-measuring the picture and adjusts its contrast over time, which showed as brightness and saturation shifting during play. In the original it is on only at Beautiful and Fantastic (`renderLevel > 4`), and Fantastic adds SSAO. `Settings.updatePrefs()` now enables contrast stretch only above Good and sets `World.lodDist` as the original did. SSAO stays off, because its `Hidden/SSAO` shader does not compile on Metal. Verified in the editor at the default Good level: `Tools -> MarsXPLR -> Log Lighting Over Time` shows contrast stretch off and steady screen brightness (121–124) over 30 s of driving. The Wine floor reference above was taken at Fantastic, with contrast stretch on in both versions. The port does not yet apply `renderLevel` to `QualitySettings`.

  Laser aimer (2026-10-20): the Wine version shows a green aimer while not locked on. `Vehicle.prefab` (binary) references exactly two particle materials, `Plasma Green` and `Plasma Red`, for `LaserAim` and `LaserAimLocked`. The port's placeholder beam had guessed the colours the other way round. `LegacyLaserSightController` now draws the beam with the original materials (`Resources/LaserSightMaterials.asset`): Plasma Green while aiming, Plasma Red while locked. The placeholder beam still read pale rather than clearly green, so the original particle settings were recovered as follows. Later the same day: the aimer's legacy particle settings were read from the binary `Vehicle.prefab` through its Unity 4.6 type tree. `LaserAim` (Plasma Green) emits 5 particles per second at the vehicle's origin with no initial speed. Each lives 3 s, starts at size 0.05 and grows by `sizeGrow` 6, and is pushed forwards by a 200 m/s² force in local space. The renderer stretches each particle by its speed (velocity scale 0.4, length scale 1), capped at 10% of the screen. The colour animation is white with alpha 0, 0, 0.565, 0.345, 0 at 0, 25, 50, 75 and 100% of life. So the beam is transparent for about the first 56 m and shows as long streaks further out. This matches the Wine version, where the beam is barely visible near the buggy and clearer at its far end, and more visible when acceleration lifts the nose. `LaserAimLocked` (Plasma Red) differs only in size 0.1, 7 particles per second, `sizeGrow` 4 and velocity scale 0.5. Both sit at the vehicle origin with no rotation. `LegacyLaserSightController` now rebuilds both from these values. The particle objects take the aimer's layer 11, so the minimap does not draw them. Verified in the Unity `6000.3.26f1` editor while accelerating on open floor: the particles travel forwards from 3 m to about 886 m ahead, and the beam is a thin line that starts well ahead of the buggy, shows towards its far end, and is absent from the minimap. Compared by eye side by side with the Wine version on open ground on 2026-10-20: it matches.   Legacy particle formulas, read from the original player (2026-10-20): the archived Mac build of Mars Explorer (`/Applications/Games.localized/Archive/Mars Explorer.app`, i386 slice) keeps its scripting binding table. Its `UnityEngine.ParticleAnimator::get_sizeGrow` and `get_damping` getters read offsets 0x50 and 0x4c of the ParticleAnimator, and the field layout matches the serialisation function that names each field. The one function that reads both offsets (at 0x161f30) is the per-frame particle update. Each frame it multiplies each particle's velocity by `powf(damping, dt)`, adds `force * dt`, and multiplies its size by `powf(1 + sizeGrow, dt)`. So size grows exponentially: `size(t) = startSize * (1 + sizeGrow)^t`, and damping is a per-second velocity factor. For `LaserAim` the size is 0.05 × 7ᵗ m: 0.26 m at 0.85 s, 4 m at 2.25 s and 17 m at the end of its 3 s life. Particles are transparent by then. `LaserAimLocked` grows as 0.1 × 5ᵗ m. The earlier linear reading (0.05 m to 0.95 m) was wrong, and the far-end difference measured above came from it. `LegacyLaserSightController` now builds its size-over-lifetime curve from this formula.

  Verified in the Unity `6000.3.26f1` editor on macOS `27.0.1` with the same capture and measurement (18 frames): the near end is 0.073–0.085 H below the horizon. Summed brightness is within 10–25% of Wine, against two to three times lower before. The beam's colour is unchanged. The base marker glow (`LOD0`) uses the same legacy animator and is now rebuilt from these formulas (see Base marker above). A pale line that runs back from the buggy towards the chase camera appears in both versions and is not the aimer.

## HUD font

The Ethnocentric fonts were empty in every copy of the project because their data lived in classic Mac resource forks. The TrueType data was recovered from the AppleDouble sidecars in `unity/` and the fonts switched from "ASCII upper case" to Dynamic, which Unity 6's IMGUI text renderer requires. All Ethnocentric lowercase glyphs match its capitals, so presentation is unchanged. Verified in the Unity `6000.3.26f1` editor on 2026-10-15: no font warnings and HUD text renders.
